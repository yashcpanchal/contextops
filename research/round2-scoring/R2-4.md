# R2-4: Repo-specific, agent-proof hiring work samples (independent evaluation)

Evaluator date: 2026-10-13. Inputs: TOP10_R2.md, RUBRIC.md, round2/hiring_deep.md, round2/hiring.md, plus new research (about 20 fetches and helper queries). Caveat: the shared WebSearch budget ran out after 3 searches, so the rest of the new research came from WebFetch on known URLs and the HN helper. Reddit returned 429 twice.

## The idea as scored
A GitHub app generates work-sample tasks from the customer's own repo: planted bugs, ambiguous specs, "agent-bait" and review-the-agent's-PR tasks. It rotates a fresh variant for each candidate and red-teams every task against frontier agents (Claude Code, Codex) so it cannot be one-shot. The candidate directs an AI agent, and grading is on judgment. Buyer: Head of Eng or a hiring-owning staff engineer at companies with 50-1,000 engineers.

**Sharpening I tested:** position it as a content and verification engine ("agent-solvability score + leak-proof variants") rather than an assessment platform, and also sell it to assessment vendors. It does not raise the score honestly. Vendors are few, and they are building authoring tools themselves (CodeSignal Cosmo, below). I scored the original positioning.

## New demand evidence (money already spent)
- **The category has real budgets.** Vendr buyer data for HackerRank: median about $13.5k/yr, range $4.5k-$45.8k, based on 186 purchases (https://vendr.com/marketplace/hackerrank). CodeSignal: median $24k/yr, range $6.8k-$66k, 63 purchases (https://vendr.com/marketplace/codesignal). A search summary cites separate spend data from 160 customers: about $6.3k SMB and $70.6k enterprise. Karat charges about $200-450 per interview (hiring_deep.md). Technical assessment is a proven line item, usually owned by Talent Acquisition with Eng sign-off.
- **The repo-based, AI-allowed slice is now priced in public, and the prices are low.** Saffron (YC P26) charges $199/mo for 5 assessments and $499/mo for 15, with extras at $49 each and a $5 Claude Code budget per assessment. Its page shows no customer logos and no traction claims (https://www.trysaffron.ai/). That puts the slice at about $2.4k-6k/yr per small customer.
- **Buyers' own words show they want harder, realistic tasks with real bugs.** But they build those tasks themselves:
  - prateeksi, building a DEX: "a live matching engine with a subtle bug... The ones steering AI without real knowledge submit a fix that breaks three other things" (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47415117).
  - mnembrini plants bugs in a small Spring Boot app for candidates to review (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49768826, 48 pts/43 comments).
  - fbrncci runs a 2-hour AI-only task on an internal harness, "a company tool, not a public one," and rejects about 80%.
  - smashburger tried an agentic interview on an existing codebase, then dropped it because "results varied too much."
  - Tutor Intelligence published a take-home "because Claude can 1-shot our interviews" (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46557045).

  This is the core demand signal. It is also the core "they'll do it themselves" signal.
- **Job postings:** I found no postings for people who author or rotate interview content at customer companies. HackerRank is hiring ML engineers for Chakra (edtech.com/jobs listing), which means the incumbent, not the buyer, is staffing this work.
- **Funding in the exact slice is tiny.** Saffron raised about $0.5M pre-seed. Outship (YC W25) has 3 people and no published traction or pricing (https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/outship). Merge (HN Show, 4 pts) sells AI-native PR-review assessments. Utkrusht raised an undisclosed seed. The large rounds in AI hiring go to screening bots (Alex $17M, Fika $4M) and labor marketplaces (micro1, Mercor), not to repo work samples.

## Disproof attempts
1. **Incumbents are shipping it.**
   - HackerRank Chakra went GA on Oct 5 2026 after more than 500k beta interviews. It uses "real work in code repositories" with an AI assistant and adaptive follow-up questions, and HackerRank has more than 3,000 business customers (https://www.hackerrank.com/writing/why-were-sorry; runtimewire coverage). One pricing guide says Chakra is included in every plan.
   - CodeSignal (Apr 28 2026) shipped agentic authoring that "builds assessments, AI interviews, or live interview questions of all types" in minutes. It generates from a job description; its release does not mention customer repos (https://codesignal.com/newsroom/press-releases/).
   - CodeSubmit (logos include Netflix, Apple and Audi) offers real-repo take-homes, AI usage tracking, an AI Challenge Creator that drafts tasks from a role brief, and a voice check that candidates understand their own code (https://www.codesubmit.io/).
   - Hatchways (YC S19) already sells customizable GitHub-based assessments.

   The only unclaimed pieces are (a) generating from the customer's actual repo and (b) verified agent-solvability. Saffron and Outship already do (a). (b) is a feature, not a company.
2. **Platform absorption.** Generating tasks from a repo is a prompt plus a sandbox. Any of the vendors above, or Anthropic/Cursor-style "eval your hire" features, can add "planted-bug variant from your repo" in a quarter.
3. **Budget owner.** A budget exists (TA tooling, $5-25k), but it is already committed to HackerRank or CodeSignal, often with a Chakra-style AI interview bundled in. The new tool has to displace them or ask for incremental money from a Head of Eng who already plants bugs by hand.
4. **Validity skepticism.** Practitioners report inverted or noisy signal from agentic interviews (smashburger's variance; Aurornis's "inverted signals," cited in hiring_deep.md). Others reject the premise and go back to no-AI fundamentals (dakiol, jarl-ragnar, latentsea). The buyer is unsure the format works at all, so they will not pay a premium for content in it.
5. **Repo-access friction.** Companies must hand their proprietary code to a student startup that runs it in sandboxes candidates can reach. That is a security review on day 1 and conflicts with the "no enterprise sales cycle" wedge. One practical answer is to generate a sanitized or synthetic clone of the repo, but that dilutes the "your own codebase" pitch.
6. **Volume.** The ICP (50-1,000 engineers) hires perhaps 10-100 engineers a year, and post-AI hiring is lower. Saffron's pricing implies $2-6k ACV. Even at the incumbents' median ($13-24k), reaching meaningful ARR takes hundreds of logos in a market with a 3,000-customer incumbent and a billboard budget.

## Sub-scores

**A. Proven demand / market: 10/20.** Money clearly moves in technical assessment (Vendr medians of $13.5k and $24k; Karat's per-interview pricing; a $0.5-2.5B category). The trend toward AI-allowed, real-codebase formats is real (Meta, Canva, HackerRank's repositioning). But demand for this specific slice (generated from the customer's repo and agent-proof) has no revenue proof. Its public price point is $199-499/mo, and capital avoids it.

**B. Sellable: 8/20.** Practitioners already build planted-bug tasks in their own code. That is the strongest evidence of need and also the strongest evidence of in-house substitution. The existing TA budget is captured by HackerRank and CodeSignal, which now bundle AI interviews. There is no cross-system or neutrality moat that would stop a Head of Eng from asking Claude for "five buggy variants of this module." Pricing is credible only at low ACV.

**C. AI-durability: 10/20.** The argument for it: every model release raises the bar for an "agent-proof" task, so verifying solvability is a moving target that improves with better models. The argument against: the product is LLM generation plus LLM grading with no proprietary data, and the incumbents hold candidate data at scale. A durable moat would need validity data (hiring outcomes), which this idea does not collect. It is very exposed to absorption by HackerRank, CodeSignal and CodeSubmit.

**D. Evidence-backed problem: 9/15.** Many independent practitioner threads (HN 49768826, 47415117, 46557045, 47724105, 49162299; r/ExperiencedDevs threads with more than 1,000 upvotes from hiring.md), plus incumbents' own pivots, confirm that classic tests are broken and agents one-shot tasks. Survey data is mostly from vendors (Fabric 38.5%, Karat's "<30% updating"). There is no direct willingness-to-pay quote for repo-specific content.

**E. Specific, reachable buyer: 6/10.** The title is clear (Head of Eng or hiring staff engineer at a startup or scale-up) and reachable via HN and YC networks. But the real buyer of assessment tooling is often TA ops. Hiring volume per account is small and volatile, and two YC teams are already working the same YC channel.

**F. Feasibility and wedge: 9/15.** An MVP (GitHub app, sandbox, 3 task types, agent red-teaming) is buildable in weeks on public APIs. But repo access triggers security review, grading needs calibration the team cannot prove quickly, and differentiation against Saffron and Outship (same architecture, already launched) is thin. A student team has no hiring-domain credibility to sell validity.

**Total: 10 + 8 + 10 + 9 + 6 + 9 = 52.**

## Verdict
The pain is real and loud, and money does flow into technical assessment. But this exact wedge was taken in 2026 by HackerRank Chakra, CodeSignal's agentic authoring, CodeSubmit, and two YC teams (Saffron, Outship), and buyers who care most already plant bugs in their own repos for free. It scores below the round 1 winner (56). It is worth pursuing only if a design partner hiring more than 50 engineers a year commits to sharing outcome data, which would turn it into the validity-data business (Idea B in hiring_deep.md).
