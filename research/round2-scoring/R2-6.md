Evaluator: Opus, 2026-10-13. Inputs: TOP10_R2.md, RUBRIC.md, incidents.md (idea D, 57; idea B, 49), qa.md (idea D, 57), reddit_demand.md (idea B). New research: about 16 calls (WebSearch, WebFetch and the HN helper). **Limitation:** the shared WebSearch budget ran out after 3 searches, so most new evidence comes from fetching vendor pages and HN directly. I could not count job postings. The Reddit archive returned 429/422 errors on both attempts.

## The idea as stated
The product has three parts:
1. Link production incidents to the PRs that caused them, whether AI-authored or not.
2. Use that history to gate risky AI changes before merge or deploy.
3. Track postmortem action items until they are done.

Buyers: VP Eng, Head of Platform or Head of SRE, at companies with 100 to 2,000 engineers.

## New demand evidence (what I found)

**The pain is real, it is growing, and it is now quantified (mostly by vendors):**
- **Faros AI, "Speed Trap" (Sept 2026).** Based on telemetry from 22,000 developers on 4,000 teams:
  - monthly incidents up 125.4%
  - incidents per PR up 14.5% (the April report had said +242.7%)
  - 76.3% of PRs merged without review
  - time in QA up 300.6%
  - PR size up 71.8%

  Faros labels these findings correlational, and the data is vendor data. Source: https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-speed-trap-takeaways
- **Faros, "Acceleration Whiplash" (Apr 2026).** Bugs per developer up 54%, monthly incidents up 57.9%, median time to first review up 156.6%. https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways
- **Amazon, March 2026.** After outages, Amazon reportedly began requiring senior engineers to sign off on AI-assisted changes from junior and mid-level engineers. An internal briefing named "novel GenAI usage" as a contributing factor. The HN thread scored 659 points with 485 comments: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47323017 (FT/Ars Technica). This is the strongest buyer-behavior signal for the change-gating half of the idea. But the response was a *process* (human sign-off), not a purchase. One commenter observed that "Admitting AI code as the root cause of a CoE is gonna look really bad" while managers are being judged on AI adoption. That is a political headwind for any tool that attributes incidents to AI.
- **DORA 2025.** AI adoption is associated with higher delivery instability (cited in incidents.md).
- **Reddit (from reddit_demand.md).** Two threads on repeat postmortems: 398 upvotes/86 comments and 178/32. The second describes the manual process: "score them by risk... and raise hell"; "Assign money values to the incidents."

**Money already spent:** large, but on adjacent layers rather than this product:
- Incident management: incident.io (Series B, $62M at a $400M valuation), Rootly, PagerDuty (ARR $499M)
- AI SRE investigation: Resolve ($1B+ valuation), Traversal, Cleric
- Change management inside ITSM: ServiceNow sells a "Change Success Score" in ITSM Professional, which scores how likely a team's change request is to succeed based on its history: https://www.servicenow.com/docs/bundle/zurich-it-service-management/page/product/change-management/concept/change-success-score.html

So a change-risk budget exists in ITIL shops, but ServiceNow owns it.

**Counter-signals on demand:**
- **Sleuth**, the best-known deploy-tracking and DORA change-failure startup, now leads its homepage with "Sleuth Skills" (AGENTS.md and MCP governance). DORA is a secondary link (https://www.sleuth.io/). A focused vendor moving away from change tracking as its headline product suggests the standalone category was hard to monetize.
- HN has at least 8 "Show HN" postmortem and action-item tools from 2025-26 (AutoBrief, IncidentPost, BlamelessPostmortem and others), each with 1-2 points and almost no comments: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47209952 , https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46654431 . Many builders, no visible pull.

**Conclusion of the disproof.** Two of the three legs (follow-through and incident-to-change linkage) are table stakes in tools the buyer already pays for. The open gap is the third leg: a pre-merge gate informed by incident history. That gap is narrow, and it sits where the incident platforms (which own the data) and the AI code reviewers (which own the PR comment slot; CodeRabbit is valued at $1.5B) will meet. Both groups already pass production context to coding agents through MCP. An HN commenter on the Amazon thread described the DIY version: one agent template per past incident, run against each new PR (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47329494). With general models, that is a weekend script.

## Sharpened positioning (scored below)
I drop postmortem tracking, which is commoditized. I reframe the product as a **neutral "AI change escape ledger" for the VP Eng who has to defend the AI-coding rollout**. For each AI tool, agent, model and repo, it reports:
- incidents per merged PR
- revert and hotfix rate
- review depth

The pre-merge gate becomes a secondary feature that routes high-blast-radius AI PRs touching incident-prone files to a senior reviewer. This automates Amazon's policy rather than inventing a new one.

Why this is a better framing:
- It is cross-vendor: Cursor, Claude Code and Copilot each report only on themselves, and incident tools do not know which agent wrote the code.
- It fits as a module of the ContextOps spend-governance story: ROI = spend vs. escapes.

The honest effect is about +1. The Faros, LinearB, DX and Jellyfish class can add an "incidents per AI PR" chart with little effort, and Faros already ingests incident tools.

## Scores (sharpened version)

**A. Proven demand / market: 10/20.** Two things are true. The pain is quantified and growing: Faros reports +125% monthly incidents, DORA reports more instability, and Amazon changed policy publicly. Money flows heavily into incident tooling. But I found no money flowing into *this* product. The focused precursor (Sleuth) moved its headline away from change tracking. Postmortem tools on HN get no traction. ServiceNow's change-risk score shows that budget exists only inside ITSM suites.

**B. Sellable: 7/20.** The cross-system join (incident tool, git, AI-tool attribution) is real. But the buyer already pays incident.io, Rootly or PagerDuty, and those platforms bundle follow-ups and change correlation. There is no distinct budget line: it would come out of eng-intelligence or incident budgets, where incumbents sit. Pricing is unclear; my guess is $10-20/dev/month, competing against bundled features. The political headwind (no one wants to name AI as the root cause) slows purchase.

**C. AI-durability: 9/20.** The moat is labeled incident-to-PR history and AI attribution, not the LLM. That part is good. But the owners of that data (incident platforms, observability vendors, eng-intel tools) are each one feature away. AI code reviewers can take in incident context through MCP. Better models make the DIY "agent per past incident" approach cheaper. The risk of platform absorption within a year is high.

**D. Evidence-backed problem: 9/15.** The sources are independent of each other: DORA (n≈5,000), Faros telemetry (vendor), FT/Ars reporting on Amazon, two Reddit threads, and several HN threads. The weakness is that most quantification is vendor-sourced, and there is no "I'd pay" statement anywhere.

**E. Specific, reachable buyer: 6/10.** The titles are clear: VP Eng and Head of Platform at 200-2,000 engineers. That group is reachable via LinkedIn and LeadDev, but it is the most heavily solicited buyer in dev tooling. Smaller teams have too few incidents to produce a meaningful signal, so the reachable SMB segment does not have the problem in measurable form.

**F. Feasibility & wedge: 9/15.** A GitHub App plus the PagerDuty/incident.io/Sentry APIs is buildable in weeks. AI authorship can be detected from co-author trailers and bot accounts. The hard parts:
- Incident-to-PR linkage is sparse and noisy in real data.
- A risk gate needs months of history before it is useful (cold start).
- Proving accuracy needs design partners with real incident volume, which means mid-size companies, which means a security review.

Differentiation from incumbents is thin.

Unsharpened original (all three legs): A 10, B 6, C 8, D 9, E 6, F 8 = 47. Sharpened: 10 + 7 + 9 + 9 + 6 + 9 = **50**.

## Verdict
This is a real, growing pain with weak pull for a standalone product: the buyer's existing incident and eng-intelligence vendors own the data and ship most of the features. It is best kept as an "escape rate" module inside ContextOps, not as a company. It does not beat round 1 (56).
