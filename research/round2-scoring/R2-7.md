# R2-7: Capitalization and Section G evidence builder sold through CPA / R&D-credit firms

Evaluator: independent Opus scorer. Date: 2026-10-13. Source idea: `research/round2/engfinance.md`, Idea C (scout self-score 55; shortlist range 55-58).

## The idea as given
White-label SaaS for regional CPA firms and R&D-credit boutiques. It pulls Jira/GitHub/Linear evidence for each business component (technical uncertainty, experimentation, qualified time) and exports Form 6765 Section G plus capitalization schedules. It is priced per study ($500-3,000) or per seat. It is not AI-specific.

## Sharpened version (what I scored)
I changed three things. They make the idea more honest, not more attractive.
1. **Drop ASC 350-40 capitalization from the channel pitch.** The client's auditor cannot build the client's capitalization evidence without independence problems. The controller buys that product directly, and funded tools already cover it: Jellyfish DevFinOps, DX's "R&D Capitalization" product (DX is now owned by Atlassian), and Neo.Tax.
2. **Target only clients that must file Section G.** Under the IRS instructions, Section G is required for tax years beginning after 2025. The exceptions are qualified small businesses taking the payroll election, and taxpayers with **QREs of $1.5M or less AND average gross receipts of $50M or less, filing on an original return** ([IRS i6765](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i6765)). Smith + Howard notes that amended-return claims get no such exemption ([Smith + Howard](https://www.smith-howard.com/section-g-why-rd-tax-credit-claims-are-getting-more-complicated/)). The real market is therefore software companies with roughly 12+ engineers' worth of QREs, plus anyone filing amended or refund claims. Seed-stage startups, a large part of a regional CPA's book, are exempt.
3. **Sell as an "engineering-evidence connector" to R&D-credit practice leaders, not as a white-label dashboard.** Questionnaire-style white-label R&D software for CPAs already exists (see below). The only defensible wedge is the GitHub/Jira-to-business-component mapping.

Scoring the sharpened version raised D and F slightly and lowered A slightly, because the target market is smaller. The net effect is roughly neutral.

## New demand research

**Money already moving in this market (strong).**
- Advisors commonly take contingency fees of 15-25% of the credit (engfinance.md). Taxnova cites more than $32bn a year in US federal R&D credits ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2026/02/04/taxnova-joins-a16z-speedrun-after-1m-pre-seed-raise/)).
- Strike claims 1,100+ clients ([Strike](https://www.striketax.com/journal/form-6765-section-g-becomes-mandatory-in-2026-most-ai-only-r-d-credit-tools-will-not-be-ready)).
- Boast says it has "100+ accounting firms, development agencies, and business advisors" as referral partners ([Boast partners](https://boast.ai/partners/)).
- Onshore raised a $31M Series B in Feb 2026 (engfinance.md).

**The CPA channel already buys R&D-credit software (strong, and a double-edged signal).**
- KBKG's **Dash.tax** is marketed "for CPAs": a white-labeled dashboard and reports with the firm's branding, client self-entry of R&D data, and "a single fee per completed claim." Small businesses pay 5% of the credit, and CPAs can add an optional 3% audit-support fee ([dash.tax](https://dash.tax/); [KBKG software](https://www.kbkg.com/software)).
- Clarus R+D runs a "Tax Practitioner" track ([Clarus](https://www.clarusrd.com/)).
- This proves both a budget line and a price anchor. It also proves that the plain white-label idea is taken. Neither Dash.tax nor Clarus mentions GitHub/Jira integration or Section G on its site, and that gap is the opening.

**Section G is the forcing function (verified primary).**
- Section G requires, for each component, a narrative and wage QREs split into direct research, supervision and support, along with supplies, computer rental and contract research. Components covering 80% of QREs must be listed, up to 50. Software components must be classed as IUS, DFS, Non-IUS or excepted ([IRS i6765](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i6765)).
- The IRS burden estimate for "all other taxpayers" is 10 hr 31 min of recordkeeping and 5 hr 6 min of preparation. That is small per form, and it weakens the "huge pain" narrative.
- The practitioner view is stronger. Source Advisors' Deborah Roth (4,500+ studies) writes that straightforward R&D credit filings are "largely over" ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/03/13/navigating-form-6765-what-cpas-must-know-for-rd-tax-compliance/157320/)).
- Smith + Howard says claims built "on anything except contemporaneous documentation are increasingly exposed."
- A practitioner podcast notes that generalist CPAs usually do not run business-component analysis; it is a specialist lane (search summary, [buzzsprout](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2618665/episodes/19487457-before-you-file-an-r-d-credit-in-2026-check-these-4-rules-episode-10)).
- That last point matters most. Regional CPAs tend to **refer** R&D work to specialists such as Boast, Source and KBKG rather than produce it themselves. The CPA "channel" may therefore be a referral network that already has an owner.

**Data-collection burden.**
- Taxnova claims data collection is about 75% of claim time [vendor] ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2026/02/04/taxnova-joins-a16z-speedrun-after-1m-pre-seed-raise/)).
- Neo.Tax quotes Thomson Reuters saying the product "significantly reduced the number of interviews we conducted" ([neo.tax](https://www.neo.tax/)).
- These are vendor claims, but they are consistent with each other.

**Buyers' own words.** These are thin. r/taxpros has relevant threads, but I could only read their titles and snippets because Reddit and the Arctic Shift archive returned 403/429:
- "How are your R&D tax credit filings going? ... how many of you folks are doing the RDTC studies in-house as opposed to outsourced" (6 points).
- "Avoiding contingent fees with R&D tax credit preparation," from a CPA who runs an R&D function at an outsourced finance firm (7 points).
- "Any tax area still not touched by AI? ... startups that use AI to automate R&D credit documentation" (15 points, 29 comments).

Engagement is low. Nobody said "I'd pay $X for a GitHub connector."

**Job postings.** Hiring is concentrated in the vendors and Big-4 delivery teams:
- Neo.Tax posted on HN in June 2026: "automates R&D tax credits and software cost capitalization for enterprises," "real revenue," customers Mercury, Brex, Notion, Adobe and Capital One ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48362254)).
- Deloitte and EY post R&D software-specialist roles (engfinance.md).
- I found no CPA-firm postings for "engineering data" tooling. That suggests firms solve this with people, not software budgets.

## Disproof attempts
- **Direct incumbents with GitHub/Jira evidence trails are already live:**
  - Neo.Tax: Jira, GitHub, Linear, Confluence and payroll integrations; ASC 350-40 and §174 outputs; enterprise logos.
  - Taxnova: GitHub, Jira, Linear, GitLab, Bitbucket and Azure DevOps; a16z Speedrun; a completed pilot with a "Top 5 R&D tax advisory firm"; positioned as "complementary to existing tax advisers," which is exactly this channel thesis.
  - CodeROI: on Aug 5, 2026 it announced GitHub-based "automated evidence for the IRS's new project-level R&D reporting" with output delivered to the client's CPA ([press release](https://pr.timesenterprise.com/article/CodeROI-Platform-Enhancement-Automated-Evidence-for-the-IRSs-New-Project-Level-RandampD-Reporting-ahead-of-2026-Mandate/6a731f919ca6f9e041dc81ef)).
  - Conclusion: the core wedge is no longer unclaimed.
- **Specialty firms do the work in-house.** Cherry Bekaert says "every engagement produces a Form 6765 Section G-ready workbook" and that it has built studies at the component level "from the start" ([CBH podcast](https://www.cbh.com/insights/podcasts/life-sciences-rd-tax-credits-form-6765-changes/)). The firms most able to pay already have a process. The firms without one tend to refer the work out.
- **Platform absorption on capitalization.** DX, owned by Atlassian, sells "R&D Capitalization" ([getdx.com](https://getdx.com/)). Jellyfish sells DevFinOps. Capitalization through a CPA channel is the weakest part of the original pitch.
- **The budget owner is ambiguous.** The CPA firm pays only if the tool lifts study margin. The client's engineering team must grant repo access to a third-party vendor of its accountant, which is a three-party trust problem. The client CFO often prefers the specialist's contingency deal because it has no upfront cost.
- **Seasonality and slow adopters.** Study work clusters around filing deadlines (first filing season for mandatory Section G: early-to-late 2027). CPA firms buy tech slowly and through peer references.

## Scores

**A. Demand (20): 11.** Large, proven spend on R&D-credit studies, and a proven CPA-channel software purchase (Dash.tax per-claim fees, Boast's 100+ accounting partners). Against that, there is no evidence that CPA firms pay specifically for engineering-system evidence. The Section G exemption removes small clients, and the specialists who would pay already produce Section G workbooks manually.

**B. Sellable (20): 10.** Small and mid-sized CPA firms clearly won't build GitHub/Jira connectors, and per-study pricing is credible against Dash.tax's anchor. But the channel tends to refer R&D work out rather than buy tools. Direct-to-company vendors (Neo.Tax, Taxnova, CodeROI) hand the CPA the output for free. The three-party data-access problem slows every deal.

**C. AI-durability (20): 10.** The moat would be connectors, the business-component mapping, and an evidence store that survives exam. LLM narrative drafting is commoditized and is what everyone already markets. Coding-assistant makers won't absorb this, but well-funded vertical players and Atlassian/DX already have the integrations. A better model helps incumbents equally.

**D. Evidence (15): 8.** The regulatory facts are verified against primary IRS text. Practitioner articles from three firms agree that the burden is rising. All of them sell the service, I found no independent buyer quotes about tooling, and the IRS's own burden estimate (about 18 hours in total) is modest.

**E. Buyer (10): 6.** The title is nameable: R&D-credit practice leader at a regional CPA firm or boutique. They are reachable via LinkedIn, AICPA/state-society events and Dash.tax-style partner lists. But a student team has no tax credibility, and the buyer is conservative and seasonal.

**F. Feasibility (15): 9.** A GitHub/Jira connector with heuristic business-component clustering and a Section G Excel export is a credible 4-6 week MVP on public APIs. The hard parts are not the code: tax-technical correctness (IUS/DFS classification, the 65% contract rule), liability when a credit is disallowed, getting design-partner repo access through an accountant, and differentiating from Taxnova and CodeROI, which shipped the same pitch in 2026.

## Verdict
The money is real and the Section G deadline is verified. But the specific gap this idea needs, engineering-evidence tooling sold through CPAs, was claimed in 2026 by Taxnova (which partners with advisors), CodeROI and Neo.Tax. Meanwhile, the CPA channel itself mostly refers R&D work to specialists who already produce Section G workbooks. The idea is mediocre: slightly below the round 1 winner, with a better forcing function but a worse competitive and channel position.

Strongest demand signal: KBKG's Dash.tax already sells white-label R&D-credit software to CPAs at a per-claim fee (5% of credit, plus 3% for audit support), which shows the channel has a budget line. Biggest risk: Taxnova, CodeROI and Neo.Tax already ship GitHub/Jira-to-R&D evidence, and CPA firms tend to refer rather than buy.

Research limits: my web-search budget ran out partway through, so later checks relied on direct page fetches. Reddit thread bodies were unreadable (403/429), so buyer quotes are limited to titles and snippets. I did not verify Neo.Tax's or Taxnova's Section G export features or prices.

FINAL SCORE: 54/100
