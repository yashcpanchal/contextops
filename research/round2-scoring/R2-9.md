# R2-9 scoring: deploy-request intake for AI-built shadow apps

Evaluator: independent scorer, 2026-10-13. Inputs: TOP10_R2.md (R2-9, pre-score 50), RUBRIC.md, round2/shadow_apps.md, round2/reddit_demand.md, plus new research. Research constraint: the session-wide WebSearch budget ran out after 3 searches, so most new evidence comes from direct WebFetch of vendor docs, sites and HN items (about 15 calls), plus the HN/Reddit helper. The Reddit archive returned 429/422 both times I tried it, and reddit.com cannot be fetched, so I could not read comment-level buyer quotes.

## The idea as given
An IT/platform "front door": an employee submits a vibe-coded app (repo, zip or URL). The tool scans it for secrets, auth model, data exposure and dependencies, writes a plain-language risk report, and routes approve/sandbox/reject through Slack or Jira. Optionally it adds an SSO-wrapped hosting target.

## What new research found

### 1. The platforms have already shipped the core of it (the biggest finding)
- **Lovable** (docs, Security page) runs a **Quick scan automatically in the publish dialog**. It covers database access rules/RLS, dependencies and MCP exposure. A **Deep scan** covers access control, unsafe input, leaked secrets, payments, auth and sensitive data. There is a workspace **Security center** (Business/Enterprise), **scheduled scans** (Enterprise), auto-fix defaults, and a **"Block publishing with critical issues"** control that is on by default for new Enterprise workspaces. Wiz (SCA/SAST) and Aikido (AI pentest) plug in natively. Source: https://docs.lovable.dev/features/security ; https://docs.lovable.dev/integrations/wiz
- **Replit Enterprise**: a Security Agent runs **SAST/SCA before publish** (Semgrep, HoundDog plus LLM reasoning), Auto-Protect watches published apps for new CVEs, and a Security Center lets admins act on vulnerabilities across all org apps in bulk. It also has RBAC on who can deploy, private deployments, SSO/SCIM and audit logs. Source: https://replit.com/enterprise
- On top of Lovable's Workspace Insights (July 2026, from shadow_apps.md), the two largest builders now own "scan before publish + admin gate + org-wide findings". Neither shows an explicit human sign-off workflow, which is the only gap left inside those platforms, and it is a small feature for them to add.

### 2. Funded third parties are explicitly pitching "govern all AI builders"
- **Pluto Security** ("AI Workspace Security Platform... oversee and protect all AI building ventures in your organization") lists Lovable, Replit, Base44, Cursor, Claude Code, v0 and Retool, and has SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001. Funding was not found. https://pluto.security/
- **Nokod Security has rebranded to Kanopy Security**. It covers LCNC/citizen-developer governance, vulnerability detection, "one-click remediation" for business users and shadow AI, on Power Platform, ServiceNow, UiPath, Retool and Claude. It claims Fortune 500 customers (anonymized). https://kanopysecurity.com/
- **Backslash** ($19M Series A, Feb 2026) covers developer IDEs and agents, not citizen builders. https://www.backslash.security/press-releases/backslash-security-raises-19m-series-a-to-secure-vibe-coding-boom-in-the-enterprise-bolsters-board-with-cybersecurity-industry-leader
- Zenity ($125M C), Nudge ($22.5M A) and Torii, from the source files, sit one sprint away. No search found a dedicated "vibe-coded app" module from Nudge, Torii or Zenity yet. https://yespress.io/nudge-security ; https://www.toriihq.com/research/apps/vibecodeapp
- **Cloudflare OS** (HN item 49183266) is a per-org sandboxed app platform with Gatekeeper approvals, audit logs and data-sensitivity policies. It is an early sign that "governed runtime" is also being absorbed at the infrastructure layer.

### 3. Demand evidence (money, budgets, buyers' words, jobs)
- **Price anchor, which is low**: Aikido, the scanner Lovable integrates, costs $0 (free tier), $300/mo or $600/mo for 10 users and 100-200 repos. Pentests start around $4,000. https://www.aikido.dev/pricing . A standalone intake/scan tool sold to mid-market IT competes with this price and with free Semgrep/Gitleaks.
- **Analyst framing supports the exact product shape**: the CSA research note (June 2026, AI-assisted and not formally reviewed) recommends a "lightweight registration process", three-tier risk classification, mandatory scanning before deploying apps that touch regulated data, and monitoring for unregistered apps. It also warns that heavy approval gates get circumvented. It reports **no data on how many organizations have such a process or budget**. Cited stats: IBM, 63% of 600 organizations have no AI governance policy; shadow-AI breaches cost about $670k extra; 5,600 vibe-coded apps scanned had no CSRF/security headers; Claude Code co-authored commits leaked secrets at 3.2% vs 1.5%. https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-vibe-coding-ai-governance-gap-20260602-csa/
- **Buyers' words**: r/sysadmin keeps producing high-engagement threads, re-verified via helper: "Vibe-coded app deployment requests from end users" 293/180; "how are you dealing with them" 269/234; COO HTML dashboard 1,029/347; "Where are you hosting your companies vibe coded stuff" 12/24; and a head of IT owning the problem in "Another vibe coder story" (6/7). On HN, an infra engineer says he is stuck maintaining PoCs that became production-critical, "the perfect nightmare". He adds that the old security argument for migrating shadow tools works far worse against vibe-coded apps (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48270943). All of this is pain and resignation. **Not one "we'd pay for X" or budget statement** was found, consistent with both source files.
- **Job postings**: none found. The single search for citizen-developer/AI-app governance roles returned only commentary, and LinkedIn/Indeed could not be fetched. This remains a gap.

## Attempts to disprove
1. **Incumbent shipping it**: confirmed for platform-hosted apps (Lovable, Replit). The scan-at-publish wedge is gone for the two largest builders.
2. **Platform absorption**: Wiz and Aikido now reach these apps *through* the builder's integration marketplace. Security vendors therefore get distribution without a new intake tool.
3. **Budget owner**: IT/sysadmin managers are the people hurting. Their observed response is policy ("they're not getting deployed"), and they lack AppSec budgets. Security owns the money but buys Zenity, Nudge, Wiz or Pluto-class platforms, not an intake form.
4. **In-house substitute**: ServiceNow/Jira request forms plus a Semgrep/Gitleaks CI job plus a Claude review prompt gives about 70% of the value for free.

## Sharpened positioning (scored below)
Platform-hosted apps are covered, so the only honest gap is **apps that leave the platforms**: Claude Code/Cursor/ChatGPT output, Python/PHP tools, and HTML on SharePoint that end users ask IT to *host* (exactly the r/sysadmin "install Python, give me a server" and "localhost" posts). Sharpened R2-9: a **"hosting request" intake for IT at 200-2,000-person companies**. It does CSA-style tiered registration, automated scanning of the uploaded artifact, a plain-language summary for the IT reviewer, an approval record, and a pull of Lovable/Replit Security Center findings for completeness. This keeps the idea out of the platforms' lane. It also narrows the market to IT teams that would self-host user apps at all, and many of them say no.

## Scores (sharpened version)

**A. Demand / market: 10/20.** Pain is real, growing and highly engaged across multiple threads. The money is real but adjacent (Zenity $125M, Nudge $22.5M, Backslash $19M) or captured by platforms (Lovable and Replit building it themselves validates the need but takes the spend). No direct budget, pricing or paid tool for "IT intake of AI-built apps" was found. The only price anchors are scanners at $0-600/month.

**B. Sellable: 7/20.** Every scanning component is free or bundled. The intake workflow fits existing ServiceNow/Jira forms. Platform-native gates cover the hosted segment. The residual cross-tool and self-hosted neutrality is worth something, but the primary user's default answer is "no, don't deploy it". People who say no do not buy a yes-machine.

**C. AI-durability: 8/20.** The "read this repo and write a risk report" core is exactly what Claude Code or a Copilot agent does on request, and it improves with every model release, which helps substitutes rather than us. Durable parts (registry, audit trail, approvals) are thin workflow, contested by Pluto, Kanopy and Zenity, and absorbable by Lovable/Replit (Lovable already blocks publish on critical findings).

**D. Evidence-backed problem: 9/15.** Independent sources: several r/sysadmin threads of 269-1,762 upvotes, HN practitioners, RedAccess/Escape scans, the CSA note, and IBM/Gartner surveys. It loses points because the evidence is of *exposure and annoyance*, not of an unmet need someone pays to fill. There are no buyer quotes about tools and no job-posting evidence.

**E. Buyer: 5/10.** The named title (IT manager/sysadmin lead, 200-2,000 employees) is reachable via r/sysadmin and MSP communities, which suits a student team. But that buyer has weak budget authority, and the funded buyer (CISO) is courted by well-capitalized platforms.

**F. Feasibility / wedge: 10/15.** An MVP in 3-4 weeks is credible (upload, Semgrep/Gitleaks/OSV, LLM summary, Slack approval). Selling a *security* gate to IT invites trust and SOC 2 questions early. It is also differentiated from Lovable/Replit only by covering apps outside them, which is the segment with the least structure to integrate with.

**Total: 10 + 7 + 8 + 9 + 5 + 10 = 49.** That is just under the 50 pre-score and below the round 1 baseline of 56. The unsharpened version, which scans platform-hosted apps, would score lower, around 45, given Lovable's and Replit's shipped pre-publish scanning.

## Verdict
The pain is real and growing, but the product has been absorbed from two sides: the builders now scan and gate publishing themselves, and funded security platforms (Pluto, Kanopy, Zenity, Wiz via integrations) are claiming the cross-tool governance layer. What remains is a low-budget IT workflow that a form and a free scanner largely replace. Pursue it only if 10 IT-manager interviews show willingness to pay above about $300/month for self-hosted-app intake. Current evidence does not show that.
