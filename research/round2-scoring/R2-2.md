# R2-2 evaluation: Automated tech due-diligence tier ($1.5-5K) for small M&A, PE and search-fund deals

Evaluator: independent Opus scorer (YC partner + ex-VP Eng/CFO lens). Date: 2026-10-13.
Inputs: TOP10_R2.md, RUBRIC.md, round2/outsourcing_dd.md (idea B, scout self-score 62), plus new research below.
Research note: the session-wide WebSearch budget ran out after 3 searches. The remaining work used about 15 direct WebFetch calls to vendor, marketplace and HN pages, plus the HN helper. Reddit returned 429 errors twice and I dropped it. Nothing below is fabricated. Items marked [vendor] are self-reported marketing.

## 1. What the idea is

The idea is a read-only repo scan (plus Jira) that produces a plain-English diligence report on code quality, security and licences, key-person/bus-factor risk, review and release process, and AI-code provenance, optionally with a human sign-off. It would be priced at $1.5-5K for deals that consultancies' $25K+ minimums price out: search funds, micro-PE, angel groups, and SMB acquirers of sub-$10M SaaS.

## 2. Demand evidence (new research)

**Money is clearly spent on tech DD, but mostly above the target tier.**
- MEV quotes searchers a fixed fee of $5K-$30K over 2-4 weeks and argues that searchers "rarely have an in-house CTO" [vendor] ([mev.com](https://mev.com/blog/technical-due-diligence-for-search-funds-and-eta-acquisitions)). Kolonell puts NY SaaS acquisitions at $8-25K, with code/architecture review alone at $3-8K [vendor] ([kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/technical-due-diligence-audit-cost-saas-acquisition-new-york-2026)). Empat's rule of thumb is 0.3-0.7% of deal size [vendor].
- The scale at the top end is real. Sema says it has "analyzed 2,700 tech companies for investors over 9 years, $2.7T enterprise value", that "16 of the 20 largest PE firms are clients", and it now sells "AI Defensibility" and "AI Adoption" products [vendor] ([semasoftware.com](https://www.semasoftware.com/)). In 2026 Black Duck Audit Services analyzed 947 codebases across 197 M&A transactions: 68% had licence conflicts and 87% had vulnerabilities ([Black Duck OSSRA](https://www.blackduck.com/resources/analyst-reports/open-source-security-risk-analysis.html)). That is strong evidence that findings exist and that buyers pay for audits.
- A new "why now" exists, though so far it rests on a single source. An HN commenter says: "Acquirers are now routinely asking about AI tool usage in development and running license scans as a condition of closing," which creates "a representation and warranty problem" ([HN 47933877](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47933877)). SIG's 2026 PE report says only a minority of organisations know where their AI-generated code sits [vendor] ([SIG](https://www.softwareimprovementgroup.com/blog/ai-and-private-equity/)).

**Volume and willingness to pay in the target tier are weak.**
- The core search-fund population is small. Stanford tracks about 850 funds since 1996 with a 58% acquisition rate, a median deal of $16M, and software as one of the top three industries but with no share given ([Stanford GSB 2026](https://gsb.stanford.edu/insights/search-funds-keep-offering-proven-path-ownership)). The 2024 study summary reports about 29 acquisitions in the latest period ([searchfunder summary](https://searchfunder.com/post/stanford-2024-search-fund-study-highlights)). That is tens of software deals a year, not thousands. At a $16M median, these buyers can and do pay the $10-30K consultants.
- Below that, the deals are too small. Acquire.com reports 2,000+ startups sold for $500M+ in total ([acquire.com](https://acquire.com/)), an average of about $250K per deal, so a $3K audit would be more than 1% of the price.
- The best buyer-voice signal I found is a self-funded searcher (<$1M EBITDA SaaS target) asking Searchfunder for "reasonably priced technical due diligence". He names Southport, Rapid Diligence and DueDilio, quotes no budget, and got 2 replies ([searchfunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/technical-dd-code-infrastructure-controls-on-a-saas-company)). The need is real but not loud.
- An instructive analog is quality of earnings (QoE). Rapid Diligence sells QoE to the same SMB buyers at $11.2K-$16.5K and has done 250+ deals in 5 years ([rapiddiligence.com](https://www.rapiddiligence.com/)). QoE is paid for because SBA lenders and investors require it. Nothing comparable requires tech DD on small deals, which is the missing forcing function. DueDilio, a marketplace with 1,300+ projects submitted, already routes the occasional tech-DD request to freelancers for a one-time fee of $149 ([duedilio.com](https://www.duedilio.com/)).
- Job postings for this work run at consultancies, not SMB buyers. Crosslake's "Technical Due Diligence Consultant" role is a freelance contract with no mention of AI or automation ([workingnomads](https://www.workingnomads.com/jobs/technical-due-diligence-consultant-crosslake-technologies-llc-823491)). Buyers outsource this work and do not staff for it.

## 3. Attempt to disprove: the tier is already occupied and prices are collapsing

This is the decisive finding. The proposed $1.5-5K automated tier already exists, and some offerings sit below it.
- **CodeDD** offers pay-per-audit at **EUR 4,490 flat up to 1M LoC**, plus a portfolio-monitoring plan for PE funds ([codedd.ai/pricing](https://codedd.ai/pricing)). That is exactly the proposed price.
- **CODEEGO** (London, founded 2026) sells certified codebase valuations for investors and M&A at **GBP 199 / 599 / 1,999 per assessment, with turnaround "under 20 minutes"**, plus a GBP 3,500/month enterprise plan ([codeego.com](https://codeego.com/)).
- **Borehole**, built by an ex-Amazon DD practitioner, runs "100+ deterministic checks" on git history, cites a commit for every finding, keeps private repos local, and is free for public repos ([HN 49954718](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49954718)). That is effectively the MVP proposed here.
- **Energent.ai** markets AI tech-DD agents to M&A, VC and PE teams ([energent.ai](https://www.energent.ai/use-cases/nl/tech-due-diligence)). **Sema** has moved into AI-defensibility scoring with a 2-day turnaround.
- None of these shows meaningful traction. Each Show HN got 1-2 points and no funding was found. Either the market is early or it is thin, and the evidence (low volume, no forcing function) points to thin.
- On platform absorption: a searcher with repo access can point Claude Code or Cursor at the repo and get 70% of an automated report for about $20. What remains defensible is attestation, meaning a credentialed party whose report a lender, LP or R&W insurer accepts. A student team cannot supply that.
- Two structural problems remain. Repo access arrives late (post-LOI, through the seller's counsel), and the buyer is episodic (1-10 deals in a lifetime). That gives no recurring revenue and a high CAC per report.

## 4. Sharpening attempted

The strongest honest repositioning is a **sell-side "exit-readiness + AI-code provenance attestation"** for $1-10M ARR SaaS founders, sold through M&A brokers and marketplaces (FE International, Quiet Light, Acquire.com). The repo owner is the buyer, so access is not a problem. The report doubles as a data-room artifact, brokers bring repeat deal flow, and "show where AI-written code sits and that licences are clean" answers the rep-and-warranty question raised above. I found no evidence that brokers or sellers pay for this today, CODEEGO already targets founders "proving software value", and attestation still needs credibility. The sharpening improves buyer reachability (E) and feasibility (F) by about 1 point each and drags evidence the other way. I score the original idea and treat the sharpened version as a roughly 50-point variant that is not demonstrably better.

## 6. Verdict

This is a real but small and mostly episodic market. Its high-value segment is owned by trust-based consultancies (Sema, Crosslake, Black Duck, SIG), and the low-cost automated tier this idea targets already has at least four entrants, priced from free to EUR 4,490, with no visible traction. It scores below the round 1 winner (56). Its one live hook, AI-code provenance as a rep-and-warranty question, is better used as a feature of R2-1/R2-3-style evidence products than as a standalone DD business.

FINAL SCORE: 46/100
