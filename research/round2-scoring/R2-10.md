# R2-10: Flaky-test cost triage with auto-fix PRs

**Idea (from TOP10_R2.md, qa.md Idea C, moneyflow.md Rank 1 idea 1):** a tool that measures what flaky tests cost in CI minutes and engineer hours, then auto-quarantines flaky tests and opens auto-fix PRs. Source score: 53 (qa.md: A10 B8 C5 D10 E7 F13).

**Method note:** about 15 web calls (6 WebSearch, then the session-wide search budget ran out, so I switched to WebFetch on known URLs) plus the HN helper. The Reddit archive returned HTTP 429 twice. Demand evidence came mainly from vendor docs, pricing pages, engineering blogs and HN. I found no job-posting data beyond Trunk's own hiring.

## 1. What the new research found

### Demand evidence (money being spent)
- **The category has a funded specialist.** Trunk raised a $25M Series A in April 2022 (a16z, Initialized), about $28.5M in total ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/trunk-technologies), [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/trunk)). I found no new round since. Its Flaky Tests product left beta on 1 Oct 2025 ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45451786)). Trunk lists Brex, Ramp, Retool, PostHog, Gusto, Klaviyo and Cockroach Labs as customers. A Brex engineer said they had "no visibility" into how many flaky tests existed ([trunk.io/insights](https://trunk.io/insights)). Trunk was still hiring a "Tech Lead (CI & Test Data Platform)" in Feb-Mar 2026 ([HN Who is hiring](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47251892)). This is real but modest money: a 2022 round with no visible follow-on.
- **Prices are low and per-unit.** Trunk is free for up to 5 committers, then priced per committer, with custom Enterprise pricing ([trunk.io/pricing](https://trunk.io/pricing)). Buildkite Test Engine includes 250 managed tests, then charges $0.10 per test per month ([pricing](https://buildkite.com/pricing/)). BuildPulse costs $99-499/month (qa.md). A Buildkite user refused to adopt it: "$0.10 quickly gets very expensive" ([forum](https://forum.buildkite.community/t/unhappy-with-test-suite-pricing/4572)). Willingness to pay exists, but it is price-sensitive and shaped like a tool budget, not a headcount budget.
- **Big incumbents are investing in the category.** Datadog, CircleCI, Atlassian and Trunk each shipped AI flaky-test fixing in 2025-2026 (section 2). That validates demand, but it is also the main threat.

### Pain evidence (independent, measured)
- **Atlassian:** flaky reruns in the Jira backend waste "over 150,000 hours of developer time each year". Flaky tests caused up to 21% of master-build failures in Jira Frontend. Atlassian built its own tool, Flakinator ([Atlassian blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/how-we-build/taming-test-flakiness-how-we-built-a-scalable-tool-to-detect-and-manage-flaky-tests)).
- **GitHub:** 1 in 11 monolith commits had a red build from a flaky test. Its in-house system cut that 18x ([GitHub Eng](https://github.blog/engineering/engineering-principles/reducing-flaky-builds-by-18x/)). Google reports about 16% of tests show some flakiness ([Google Testing](https://testing.googleblog.com/2016/05/flaky-tests-at-google-and-how-we.html)).
- **Bitrise:** the share of teams reporting flakiness rose from 10% (2022) to 26% (2025) across more than 10M builds ([via quashbugs](https://quashbugs.com/blog/flaky-test-statistics)).
- **Academic work:** 77% of developers call flaky tests time-consuming (n≈120). A Daimler Truck case puts the cost at ≥2.5% of productive developer time (qa.md).
- **Practitioner words:** "retries everywhere, quarantining tests... adding more waits than anyone feels good about." The hard part is telling a flake from a product race condition: "same symptom, totally different fix" ([Ask HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46967724)). thoughtbot reports a quarantined spec group that failed about 60% of CI runs ([thoughtbot](https://thoughtbot.com/blog/what-it-took-to-use-this-overnight-ai-solution)).

The pain is real, measured, and growing as AI agents push more commits into CI. An HN commenter running "~20 coding agents" called the load "rough on your CI meter" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49612773)).

## 2. Disproof attempts (these largely succeeded)

1. **Datadog already ships it.** Bits Code plus Test Optimization detects flaky tests, classifies the failure, generates a fix validated against flake history, and opens an "Attempt to Fix" PR. It can auto-push merge-ready PRs. Policies auto-quarantine tests, then disable them after 30 days unfixed. Datadog even gates fixes on "≥2 hours of wasted time", so it already measures cost ([Datadog blog, Jan 2026](https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/bits-ai-test-optimization/), [docs](https://docs.datadoghq.com/tests/flaky_management/)). Status: Preview.
2. **CircleCI already ships it.** Chunk "Fix flaky tests" runs on a schedule, generates fixes, validates them over repeated runs in isolation, and opens PRs. It is free in beta and will be paid at GA ([CircleCI docs](https://circleci.com/docs/guides/test/fix-flaky-tests), [blog Oct 2025](https://circleci.com/blog/fix-flaky-tests-with-chunk)).
3. **Atlassian Bitbucket already ships it.** "AI-driven flaky test remediation" (beta) uses a default or bring-your-own agent and raises a draft PR ([Atlassian support](https://support.atlassian.com/bitbucket-cloud/docs/ai-driven-flaky-test-remediation/)).
4. **Trunk, the cross-CI neutral player, already ships it.** Its autofix agent investigates flaky tests and "raise[s] fix pull requests" (waitlist beta), and CI Autopilot opens stacked fix PRs ([Trunk docs](https://docs.trunk.io/flaky-tests/agents/autofix-flaky-tests.md), [CI Autopilot](https://docs.trunk.io/ci-autopilot)). The cross-vendor neutrality that moneyflow.md said was needed for durability is therefore already taken by an a16z-backed company with a strong logo list.
5. **Buildkite** ships auto-quarantine and flaky workflows to Slack and Linear in its Pro plan.
6. **DIY with Claude Code works.** The Gumroad "autoresearch" post ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47602537)), thoughtbot, "Lessons Learned from Fixing Flaky Tests with Claude" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49312355)), and an HN user who fixed a race condition with a Claude debugging skill all show this. The thoughtbot post notes that raw agent fixes were noisy and needed two weeks of expert cleanup. That leaves room for a quality layer, but Datadog and CircleCI already validate fixes by re-running tests.
7. **Large companies build in-house.** Atlassian (Flakinator), GitHub, Google and Uber ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41669125)) all built their own. The buyers with the biggest pain build, and the smaller ones buy cheap tools.

**Budget owner:** the buyer is an EM, DevEx or platform lead with a tool budget. The pain is a few percent of engineering time, which rarely becomes its own line item. No CFO is asking for flaky-test dollar reports.

## 3. Sharpening attempt

The only gap I found: none of the incumbents headlines **dollar-denominated cost** (CI minutes × price + engineer rerun hours × loaded rate). Trunk's insights page has no hour or dollar figures, and Datadog uses wasted time only as a fix threshold. A sharper version would be a "CI waste ledger for agent-heavy teams": a GitHub Actions app that bills nothing until it shows "$X/month of your Actions bill and Y engineer-hours went to flakes and agent-triggered reruns," then auto-quarantines and hands off fixes to the team's own Claude Code or Copilot agent instead of competing on fix generation.

This does not change the score much. A cost report is a dashboard feature that Trunk, Datadog or GitHub can add in a sprint, and it falls back into the CI-cost-optimization space (Depot, Blacksmith, Tenki), where buyers pay for faster or cheaper runners, not for reports. I score the original idea and note that the sharpened version is worth at most +1-2 points.

## 5. Verdict

Flaky tests are a real, well-documented and growing problem, which is exactly why every CI and observability vendor shipped "detect, quarantine, AI-fix PR" in the past 12 months. A student team would be entering a feature war against Datadog, CircleCI, Atlassian and a well-funded neutral specialist (Trunk) on a low-ACV budget. Durability and sellability are near the floor. The idea scores below the round 1 winner (56) and below its own source estimate (53), because the new research found that the absorption risk qa.md predicted has already happened.

FINAL SCORE: 45/100
