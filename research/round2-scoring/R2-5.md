# R2-5 Migration Ledger: independent evaluation

Evaluator: Opus, 2026-10-13. Inputs: TOP10_R2.md, RUBRIC.md, round2/migrations.md, plus new research. The shared WebSearch budget for the session ran out after 6 searches. I added about 8 targeted WebFetch calls on primary pages and 6 HN/Reddit helper queries. Reddit returned 429 twice, so I moved on. Some background facts are labelled [background, not re-verified].

## The idea as scouted
A GitHub/GitLab App that encodes a migration (Java 8 to 21, Spring Boot 2 to 3, GCR to ECR, log4j and so on) as a detection rule. For every repo and team it shows a burn-down, nudges owners and produces an evidence-backed "done" report. It is sold to a Director or VP of Platform or Developer Productivity at companies with 500-5,000 engineers. The scout scored it 59 and admitted "no retrieved evidence of a buyer paying for tracking alone".

## New research: demand evidence
1. **Money is being spent on migration tracking, and the incumbents are getting it.** Cortex sells "Initiatives" built on Scorecards for exactly this job: "define a migration, automatically link affected components, assign owners with deadlines", with progress reports by team, service or domain and Slack/email reminders. It names database, runtime, framework, CI/CD and secrets-manager migrations as use cases ([Cortex blog](https://www.cortex.io/post/accelerating-system-migrations-with-cortex); [Cortex Initiatives](https://www.cortex.io/solutions/initiatives); [Cortex migrations docs](https://docs.cortex.io/solutions/migrations/configure)). Cortex's own framing confirms the pain: migrations "are often tracked by simple spreadsheet or project planner". That is a vendor claim, but it is the claim the idea rests on.
2. **The best buyer quote I found is a Cortex customer.** Outreach tracked its forced migration from GCR to ECR (Google deprecated GCR) across 100+ services with a Cortex "ECR Readiness" scorecard. Cortex reports 75% time saved on project management, for a dev-tooling team serving 300 engineers. Their engineer, Egor Kalinichev, said: "If we did not have anything at all, administrative methods would be our only choice." He also said: "If I had to choose between hiring one more person or purchasing Cortex, it would be an easy choice" ([Outreach case study](https://cortex.io/case-studies/outreach)). That is real willingness to pay, and it is already captured.
3. **OpsLevel Campaigns** do the same job: a filter-scoped set of services, a deadline, a burn-up chart and reminders sent only to teams that haven't finished, with a "one-time Kubernetes migration" given as the example ([OpsLevel campaigns](https://www.opslevel.com/resources/how-and-when-to-use-campaigns); [OpsLevel blog](https://opslevel.com/blog/engineering-priorities-opslevel-campaigns)). **Port** markets "migration scorecards", initiative dashboards, and Jira/GitHub issues that auto-open for violated rules and auto-close once fixed ([Port guide](https://www.port.io/guide/scorecards); [Port docs](https://docs.getport.io/promote-scorecards/manage-using-3rd-party-apps/)).
4. **Moderne DevCenter** (blog dated 2025-09-30) shows migration progress across the fleet as parliament charts, one seat per repo by version. From the same screen you can trigger upgrade recipes and track the resulting PRs to approval. It is aimed at "developers to executives" ([Moderne blog](https://moderne.ai/blog/moderne-devcenter-dashboard-used-to-migrate-secure-visualize-large-codebases)). Moderne raised a $30M Series B in Feb 2025, per the scout.
5. **Sourcegraph Batch Changes** gives a burndown of merged changesets across many repos and code hosts ([Sourcegraph docs](https://sourcegraph.com/docs/batch_changes); [migration solution page](https://sourcegraph.com/solutions/code-migrations-and-modernization)).
6. **GitHub Copilot app modernization** reached GA for Java/.NET/C++ upgrades in the IDE. A "modernization agent" CLI in public preview orchestrates "assessment, migration planning, and framework upgrade automation across multiple applications simultaneously" ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/developer/github-copilot-app-modernization/overview); [GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-05-19-github-copilot-app-modernization-for-java-now-in-public-preview/)). The platform is moving up from execution into portfolio planning.
7. **Money for EOL compliance goes to avoidance, not tracking.** HeroDevs sells Never-Ending Support for EOL open source. It claims 1,000+ customers and "50%+ of the Fortune 100", offers a **free EOL scan** of your codebase, and supplies evidence "mapped to SOC 2, PCI, HIPAA, FedRAMP, CRA, and DORA" ([HeroDevs](https://www.herodevs.com/); [about](https://www.herodevs.com/about)). That closes off the obvious compliance pivot (see below).
8. **Community voice is thin.** I ran HN comment searches for "migration spreadsheet repos", "tracking migrations across services", "long tail migration", "Java 8 upgrade hundreds of services" and "Cortex OpsLevel Backstage scorecards". They returned almost nothing on-topic. The one relevant hit is the "Ask HN: Advice for leading a software migration?" thread. One commenter there says the long tail "tends to burn engineers out, because it's boring and has virtually no real benefit". Their fix was company-wide Fixits, not a tool ([HN 40762456](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40762456)). Another opens with a PM demanding "Fill out this spreadsheet with key dates" ([HN 40763180](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40763180)). Reddit r/platformengineering "migration tracking" returned 0 posts, and r/ExperiencedDevs was rate-limited.

Context [background, not re-verified]: Atlassian announced it would buy DX, the developer-productivity insights company, for about $1B in 2025. Port and Cortex have both raised venture rounds. The developer-portal and engineering-intelligence category is funded and consolidating.

## Attempts to disprove
- **Incumbents already ship it.** All four of the most relevant vendors ship this: Cortex Initiatives, OpsLevel Campaigns, Port initiatives and Moderne DevCenter. Sourcegraph covers PR-level burndown. It is not a gap; it is a checkbox feature of developer portals, which is the budget line these buyers already use. The scout's statement that "nobody offers a vendor-neutral, cross-tool ledger" is false at the level of the feature. It is only partly true for cross-tool *verification*.
- **Platform absorption.** GitHub/Microsoft now plan modernization across many apps. AWS Transform and Claude Code do the execution. Each execution vendor will add its own progress view, as Moderne already has.
- **Missing budget owner below the IDP tier.** The 500-5,000 engineer companies the scout targets are exactly the companies that buy Cortex, OpsLevel or Port, or build on Backstage. Smaller organisations (under 300 engineers) have few enough repos that a spreadsheet or a GitHub Project is adequate, and the HN evidence shows they reach for Fixits, not software.
- **Grit precedent.** A migration startup was absorbed as a feature (Honeycomb, Apr 2025).

## Sharpening tried
**Sharpened version: an "agent-era migration proof" ledger.** It would be vendor-neutral across execution engines (Moderne, Copilot, Claude Code, Renovate PRs). It would prove "done" with *runtime* evidence (deployed artifact and JVM/.NET version taken from build/deploy telemetry, not just catalog checks) and track regressions after merge. The Bun "19 regressions after merge" thread from the scout and IBM's "translation captures almost none of the complexity" both point to verification as the durable layer. This is honestly a little better on AI-durability and differentiation, because scorecards mostly check repo and catalog state. It still competes with portal scorecards that already pull CI/CD and deploy integrations (Cortex cites 40+). It still has no independent buyer quote asking for it.

I also tested an **EOL compliance-evidence pivot** (PCI DSS 4.0's annual technology review with EOL remediation plans, plus DORA ICT asset management). HeroDevs already gives away the EOL scan and sells compliance-mapped evidence to more than 1,000 customers, so I rejected it. I score the sharpened verification version.

## Scores (sharpened version)

**A. Proven demand / market: 10/20.** Money for migration tracking clearly exists, as shown by the Outreach quote, four vendors shipping it, Moderne's $30M and the funded developer-portal category. Hard EOL deadlines (OpenJDK 8 Red Hat Nov 2026, .NET 4.6.2 Jan 2027) keep the trend alive. But the demand is already being met inside incumbent bundles. I found no standalone tracking or verification product with revenue, and I found no growth signal specific to "proof of done".

**B. Sellable: 7/20.** The decisive weakness is that the budget line ("developer portal") already belongs to Cortex, OpsLevel, Port or Backstage, all of which ship this exact workflow. Large firms build it themselves (Google, Uber GodWit). Small firms use spreadsheets or Fixits. Neutrality across execution engines is a real but thin argument. There is no credible price point, because the feature comes bundled at $0 marginal cost inside tools buyers already pay for.

**C. AI-durability: 9/20.** Cross-repo state and runtime proof are not something an LLM release replaces, which is a plus. But GitHub's modernization agent already plans across multiple apps, Moderne puts a dashboard on top of execution, and Copilot or Claude Code can produce a repo-by-repo status report on request. It is exactly the sort of feature a platform ships next. The moat would be integrations, and the developer-portal vendors already own 40+ of them.

**D. Evidence-backed real problem: 7/15.** The problem is real: the McKinsey finding that 40% of modernizations are incomplete or cancelled, DIY tooling at Google and Uber, the Outreach testimony, and HN comments on long-tail burnout. But most sources are vendor content, community complaints about tracking specifically are close to zero, and the verification angle rests on one HN thread plus an IBM PR rebuttal.

**E. Specific, reachable buyer: 5/10.** The title is named (Director of Platform or DevProd at 500-5,000 engineers). That buyer is mature, already sold to by well-funded portal vendors, and hard for a student team to reach without a warm introduction. There is no community channel where this buyer asks for help.

**F. Feasibility and wedge: 9/15.** A GitHub App MVP using ast-grep rules, lockfile scans and a burn-down is buildable in weeks with public APIs. Runtime proof needs deploy telemetry access, which means security review. The differentiation versus incumbents is weak at demo time, because a prospect will say "Cortex does this."

## Verdict
The pain is real, but it is already productized as a checkbox inside Cortex, OpsLevel, Port, Moderne DevCenter and Sourcegraph, and GitHub is moving into multi-app modernization planning. The strongest demand signal (Outreach: "an easy choice" versus hiring a person) is a testimonial for an incumbent, not a gap. The scout's 59 was too generous because it did not check developer-portal scorecards. The idea scores below the round 1 winner.

Unsharpened (pure tracker): A 10, B 6, C 7, D 7, E 5, F 9 = 44. Sharpened (agent-era verification ledger): 10 + 7 + 9 + 7 + 5 + 9 = 47.

FINAL SCORE: 47/100
