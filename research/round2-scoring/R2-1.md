## The idea as scored
ContextOps (the round 1 winner) does cross-vendor AI coding spend governance for engineering leaders at companies with 20-300 engineers. This version adds a Finance module that attributes AI spend (tokens, credits, seats) to projects and activities. It exports an ASC 350-40 capitalization schedule with a Deloitte-style evidence packet (billing record, ticket, PR, project authorization), plus §41 / Form 6765 Section G supporting schedules. It has two buyers: the VP Eng and the Controller.

## New evidence: demand (what money is moving)

**Spend governance (the Eng half) has real and growing money behind it:**
- **Weave raised a $13.5M Series A** (Jul 28, 2026; Standard Capital, YC). It measures whether AI coding spend pays off, at $50/engineer/month, across 20,000 engineers at 500+ companies including Robinhood and PostHog. Dalton Caldwell: "AI spend is the most powerful force in the world, and right now there is not an easy way to measure it." [dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/141571-weave-raises-13-5m-series-a-to-measure-whether-ai-coding-spend-pays-off/)
- **The WSJ ran "Spending on AI is becoming almost impossible for businesses to budget"** (HN: 59 points, 87 comments). In the thread, practitioners describe homegrown controls such as default monthly limits with manager review and tiered hard stops. Those are DIY workarounds, which is a demand signal but also a build-it-yourself signal. [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49964537)
- **Public-company disclosure pressure is building.** Troutman (Jun 2026) argues token costs may trigger MD&A Item 303 disclosure and cites KPMG: only 26% of companies have a comprehensive view of their AI costs. [Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/ai-token-costs-and-mda-disclosure/) Corporate Counsel (Jul 28, 2026) says analysts are asking about token expense on earnings calls, and some companies now break token spend out "with tracking controls." [thecorporatecounsel.net](https://www.thecorporatecounsel.net/blog/2026/07/earnings-risk-factors-be-prepared-to-discuss-token-expense.html)
- Earlier corpus findings still hold: Gartner's 2028 cost warning, Uber's exhausted budget, Atlassian AI wallets, and a $40K/month single-engineer bill ([beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/27/ai-coding-tool-spend-tracking-guide)).

**Capitalization and R&D evidence (the Finance half) has money behind generic capitalization, but none behind AI-token capitalization:**
- Neo.Tax's June 2026 HN hiring post names Mercury, Brex, Notion, Adobe and Capital One as customers, with "real revenue." The post does not mention AI tokens. [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48362254)
- Jellyfish (~$84K ACV) and DX both sell "R&D Capitalization" modules. DX's page ([getdx](https://getdx.com/software-capitalization/)) advertises "CPA-reviewed formulas" and "audit-ready reports" but says nothing on AI cost. Jellyfish's capitalization page has no AI-cost link either ([jellyfish](https://jellyfish.co/solutions/software-capitalization/)).
- **Buyer voice for capitalizing AI tokens is still effectively zero.** The HN archive returned one throwaway comment ("Why couldn't they capitalize token spend?", [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49978363)). The OnlyCFO piece on where AI costs belong on the P&L covers COGS/R&D/G&A classification and per-department GL coding, and never mentions capitalization ([OnlyCFO](https://www.onlycfo.io/p/where-ai-costs-belong-on-the-p-and)). The CFO-facing conversation is about **classification, forecasting and margin**, not about capitalizing tokens.

## Disproof attempts

1. **An incumbent already markets the finance feature.** Broadcom ValueOps (Apr 17, 2026) advertises Clarity ingesting usage files from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google or from internal gateways. It maps token cost to projects and tasks via charge codes so "the associated token costs are automatically routed to the balance sheet," and it stores "probability of completion assessments" ([Broadcom](https://valueops.broadcom.com/blog/accounting-best-practices-for-ai-use-in-software-development)). That is close to the exact pitch of the Finance module. It is aimed at enterprise PPM buyers, not the mid-market, but it removes any claim that "nobody does this."
2. **Platform absorption of allocation.** GitHub cost centers already charge Copilot licenses and AI-credit usage to the user, team, organization or repository's cost center, with budgets and alerts ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/billing/reference/cost-center-allocation)). Slash markets per-project virtual cards for AI spend as a pre-accounting allocation method ([Slash](https://www.slash.com/blog/how-to-classify-ai-token-spend)). Neither produces a GAAP workpaper, but they cover the "allocate by team/project" step that a Controller would use to build one in a spreadsheet.
3. **Adjacent vendors are converging.** Larridin's "Spend Intelligence" is pitched at CFOs and finance teams ("Reconcile costs across vendors"; "attributes AI costs across tools, teams, and workflows so finance can see what generated the charge") ([Larridin](https://larridin.com/), [Larridin CFO guide](https://larridin.com/blog/total-cost-ai-coding-tools-cfo)). Jellyfish lists "AI Token Cost Management" and "Software Capitalization" side by side under DevFinOps. DX has R&D Capitalization plus AI measurement. Each of the three is one join away from shipping this module.
4. **The budget owner does not match the segment.** ContextOps targets 20-300-engineer companies without an engineering-intelligence suite. Many such private companies capitalize little, and those that do are rarely audited at a level that demands token-level evidence. The companies that care most (public and PE-backed firms focused on adjusted EBITDA) are larger, already run Jellyfish, DX or Clarity, and buy through slow finance procurement. So the second buyer that justifies the Finance module mostly sits outside the segment the Eng product targets.
5. **The dollars are small, and the tax motive is weak.** This repeats ai_capitalization_verify.md §2c. At about $250/engineer/month, capitalizable tokens are about 0.6% of engineering cost. §174A already restored domestic expensing. Token QREs under §41 are contestable. Deloitte says mixed-use spend may be wholly non-capitalizable, so many controllers will rationally just expense it.

## Sharpening (scored as sharpened)
I keep ContextOps as the product and treat Finance as a **paid add-on, the "AI Spend Audit Packet."** It is not sold as a capitalization engine and makes no Section G promise. It ships three things:
1. A per-project, per-activity AI spend roll-forward, including prepaid credits.
2. Explicit flags for "mixed-use / not eligible" spend.
3. Sample-level evidence links.

Its target is the subset of ContextOps customers that are PE-backed or Series C+ and already capitalize. The pitch to the Controller shifts from "capitalize more" to "**answer the auditor's and the board's token-cost questions with evidence**." That matches the MD&A, earnings-call and classification pressure that does have evidence. Dropping the Section G angle removes the weakest claim. This adds roughly 1-2 points over the unsharpened version and is included below.

## Verdict
The Finance module is a sensible upsell and makes a better story, but it does not make a better business. Its only demand evidence is regulatory guidance, and Broadcom Clarity already markets it while Jellyfish and DX are one integration away. It lifts ContextOps by about a point, mostly through narrative and ACV potential, not proven demand. **Build the export as a cheap feature for design partners who already capitalize, ask 5 controllers and 1 audit senior before investing further, and do not position the company around it.**

Strongest demand signal: Weave's $13.5M Series A (Jul 2026) to measure AI coding spend ROI across 500+ companies, combined with analysts now questioning token expense on earnings calls. Both support the governance core, not the finance add-on.

Biggest risk: absorption from both sides. GitHub cost centers and vendor consoles take over allocation, and Broadcom Clarity, Jellyfish and DX (which already own the capitalization budget) bolt on AI-token attribution, leaving no budget line for a student-built mid-market add-on.

FINAL SCORE: 57/100
