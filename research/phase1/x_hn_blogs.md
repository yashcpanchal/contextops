# Phase 1 Research: HN, X, and Eng-Leadership Blogs/Newsletters

Researcher angle: what engineering leaders say about time sinks and how AI is changing the EM role (2025-2026). Date: 2026-10-13.

## Method and honesty notes

- **X/Twitter: not directly minable.** The `site:x.com` operator returned zero tweets. Nothing below is attributed to X. This is a gap; get X data by hand or via a scraper.

## 3. How the EM role is changing (trends that create NEW needs)

1. **Flattening plus wider spans (Gallup, Meta 50:1, Gartner).** Fewer managers with more reports means less personal knowledge per person. New need: automated context on each report's work, because the manager cannot hold it in their head. LeadDev: the multi-team EM path means "Your technical depth on any single team's work will be shallow." (verbatim)
2. **The role is splitting in two (LeadDev):** a tech-lead-manager who still codes, and a multi-team EM whose "work becomes almost entirely non-technical". Both need a lens into code and work they do not read.
3. **Managers are back in the code.** Olczyk: "Managers who used to apologise for being out of the code are now expected to be in it." Anecdotally, "the cost of getting back into a codebase has collapsed." This is the Claude Code and Copilot use case for EMs; it is already served by general tools.
4. **Review capacity is the new bottleneck.** Generation is cheap, verification is not. Needs: reviewer load balancing, risk-based routing, visibility into unreviewed or rubber-stamped merges (the 39% to 21% feedback drop).
5. **Agents as team members that need management.** Work from Claude Code, Cursor, or Codex shows up in no standup or Jira. DailyBot calls it a "visibility collapse" (vendor). Needs: attributing agent work to a human, outcome tracking, and cost per task.
6. **AI spend is a new line item an EM owns.** Pragmatic Engineer: per-engineer AI budgets are coming. EMs now have to justify and allocate token spend across a team, which no tool handles well yet.
7. **AI ROI accountability.** Boards ask "Are productivity gains offsetting costs?" (Jellyfish). Self-reported gains (64% claim 25%+) conflict with measured gains (DX about 8% throughput, METR -19%, Faros company-level flat). A neutral, credible measurement is scarce.
8. **Parallel workstreams per person.** Codacy: teams that ran one or two workstreams now juggle several per person, so WIP limiting and context-switch management falls to the EM.
9. **Expectation gap with executives** who believe AI makes everything easy (HN 46918346; Codacy).
10. **Junior pipeline shrinking** (54% expect junior hiring to decrease, LeadDev AI Impact Report, secondary) which changes mentoring and onboarding work.

## 4. Candidate startup ideas

Scoring: Demand = proven pain with evidence; Sellable = buyers will pay and won't build in-house; Durable = survives general-AI progress; Buyer = specific. Scale: H/M/L. These are my judgments, not data.

### Idea A: Agent-work attribution and visibility layer ("what did my team and their agents actually do this week")
Ingests git, PRs, Jira/Linear, CI, and agent session logs (Claude Code, Cursor, Codex) and builds a per-person and per-ticket narrative of the work, with the outcome and review status. It answers "what is this person's project?" and replaces status meetings and 1:1 prep. This is the most literal fit for the founder's Visa anecdote.
- Demand: H (visibility pain, status-report hatred, agent visibility collapse). Sellable: M (needs deep integrations and permission to read private repos; some will build a basic version in-house). Durable: M-H (the moat is cross-tool data access and agent telemetry, not the LLM summary). Buyer: Director of Eng / VP Eng, with EM as the daily user.
- **Warning from Tometo's builder:** "Because analyzing it is not directly actionable." and daily summaries were easy to build but "the hard part was judging relevance". Differentiate on agent-era data nobody else has, and on actions (draft the status report, flag blocked work), not dashboards.
- Crowded: DailyBot, Jellyfish, LinearB, Swarmia, DX, Tometo, Vereda, Ledger.

### Idea B: AI spend and ROI attribution for eng orgs (tokens to tickets to outcomes)
Tie token and seat spend to PRs, tickets, and shipped outcomes per team, with budget allocation and anomaly alerts. Directly addresses the 86% uncertainty stat, the CFO and board pressure, and Uber-style budget blowouts.
- Demand: H and rising (Pragmatic Engineer, Jellyfish, DX). Sellable: H (a CFO and VP Eng budget-line problem). Durable: M-H (needs cross-vendor data; the model vendors only show their own spend). Buyer: VP/Director of Eng plus finance partner.
- Risks: crowded with funded players (Jellyfish, DX, Faros, Exceeds AI); incumbents are adding it; "tokenmaxxing" shows metrics get gamed. This is the best-evidenced category but the least differentiated for a student team.

### Idea C: Review-capacity orchestration (route, prioritize, and audit PR review)
Measures reviewer load, predicts review debt, flags risky merges that got silent approvals, and routes by expertise. Targets the 3.1x vs 1.5x gap.
- Demand: H (CMU/Stanford, Faros, LeadDev 52%). Sellable: M (existing AI code review vendors like Qodo, CodeRabbit sit adjacent). Durable: M. Buyer: EM or Director of Eng, platform leads.
- Risk: it may be a feature of GitHub or an AI code-review product.

### Idea D: Manager "context cockpit" for wide-span EMs (1:1 prep, performance evidence, promo packets from real work)
For an EM with 12+ reports, auto-assembles per-person evidence from code, tickets, reviews, and docs, with draft talking points and review input. A superset of the 1:1 tools (Accordia, Ledger) built on real work data.
- Demand: M-H (span growth is solid; performance-review pain is widely felt but I found no strong direct quote this round). Sellable: M (EMs often buy bottom-up; HR/privacy concerns). Durable: M (ChatGPT + pasted notes is the competitor; the moat is the data pipeline). Buyer: EM, bought by Director.
- Risk: employee-surveillance backlash. Needs transparency and employee-visible views.

### Idea E: Feature and ticket cost and time forecasting in an agent era (the team's current idea)
See Section 5. Viable only as a calibrated forecast fed by real telemetry, and probably as a wedge feature, not the product.

### Idea F: Agent-aware work intake and WIP governance (what should be given to agents vs. humans, and how to cap parallel streams)
Classifies tickets for agent suitability from history (what agents succeeded at, rework rate), and enforces WIP limits. Combines the estimation idea with operational use.
- Demand: M (parallel workstreams, agent delegation decisions are emerging, thin direct evidence). Sellable: M-H if it ties to cost savings. Durable: H if built on proprietary outcome data. Buyer: Director of Eng / EM.
- Strength: this is a more defensible form of Idea E.

### Idea G: Stakeholder-facing delivery status for non-engineers (automated "where are we" for PMs/execs, from the real work)
Auto-generated, trustworthy status for leadership, replacing manual updates and the "why isn't it easy to add a button" conversations.
- Demand: M-H. Sellable: M (many Jira plugins and Notion/Linear AI already do this). Durable: L-M (commoditizing fast). Buyer: EM/PM. Weakest on defensibility; better as a surface of A.

### Idea H: Cross-vendor agent and AI-tool telemetry normalization (infrastructure for A, B, C)
A neutral data layer that normalizes sessions and costs across Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot, Codex.
- Demand: M. Sellable: M (sold to the dashboard vendors as much as to EMs). Durable: M (vendors may expose their own APIs). Buyer: eng analytics teams.
- A building block more than a product.

**Suggested ranking for a student team:** A (framed around agent-era visibility plus action) with B's cost dimension as the hook; F as the long-term moat; C as a possible second module. Validate A by interviewing 15-20 EMs with 8+ reports at companies where Claude Code or Cursor is in broad use.

## 5. Honest take on "feature/Jira ticket cost estimation"

**What is promising**
- The cost-of-agentic-work question is real and new: token costs are breaking budgets (Pragmatic Engineer), and boards ask whether productivity gains offset costs (Jellyfish).
- HN has a hint of demand for rough cost ceilings: "knowing that it will cost < $100 is actually great information even if the final work only ends up costing $5." (joshdavham)
- Tometo's builder reported that "how long should this task take" came up repeatedly from EMs.

**What is weak**
1. **Estimation is a well-trodden, low-willingness-to-pay space.** Jira Marketplace already has AI story-point tools (EstimAI, AI Story Point Estimator, AI Estimator) and Token Poker for tokens per story. Academic accuracy for story-point models is only about 71% on open-source data (one paper; versions conflict).
2. **Variance of agentic work is huge and not predictable from the ticket text.** HN: "T shirt estimation doesn't make any sense for AI dev, not one bit." The Faros/DX/METR gap suggests gains vary wildly by team and task. A per-ticket forecast will often be wrong, and a wrong number that gets turned into a commitment hurts trust.
3. **Tometo's lessons apply directly:** standalone planning features "lacked enough value to sell", analytics were "not directly actionable", and "the true market size is limited by what's actually seizable, what you can sell." (verbatim fragments)
4. **Incentive and gaming risk.** Per-ticket cost or per-employee efficiency scoring invites surveillance backlash and metric gaming (tokenmaxxing is the cautionary tale). "Employee efficiency" in the pitch is the riskiest phrase for adoption.
5. **Cost is not the manager's binding constraint.** Per the evidence, review capacity, visibility, and ROI proof are the bottlenecks; ticket-level dollar cost mostly matters to finance, and tokens per ticket are small relative to engineer salary for most tasks (Anthropic's reported average is about $6 per developer per day; that figure is second-hand).
6. **Replaceability:** a Claude or Copilot agent given Jira access can produce a rough estimate today. Only a calibrated model on private historical telemetry is defensible.

**Verdict:** Weak as a standalone product, decent as a feature. The defensible version is not "estimate the cost of a ticket" but "learn from your org's own agent run history which kinds of tickets agents complete cheaply and which blow up, then use that for routing, budgeting, and status." That requires the telemetry/attribution layer in Idea A/F first. So build the data and visibility layer, and expose estimation as a derived output once you have outcome data. Test demand cheaply: ask 15 EMs, "In the last month, did you decide who or what should do a ticket using a cost or time estimate? What did you use?" If few say yes, do not lead with it.
